# Personal Brand Strategist

## Overview

Use this skill to help someone turn scattered experiences, goals, channels, and strengths into a clear personal-brand system that can guide later content, design, website, pitch, or product work.

Default to strategy before execution. Separate what is already true, what is becoming true, and what is only a future aspiration.

Read [references/framework.md](references/framework.md) when you need the detailed diagnostic dimensions, risk checks, or deliverable recipes.

## Workflow

### 1. Ground the brand in evidence

Start from what is already true.

Collect and organize:

- Current role, work, credentials, and real proof
- Repeated experiences, projects, or environments
- Actual audience contact and field exposure
- Existing channels, accounts, and assets
- Real constraints, dislikes, and anti-goals
- Short-term focus versus long-term ambition

Do not let the brand be built from adjectives alone. Prefer evidence over aspiration.

### 2. Separate facts from ambition

Distinguish three layers:

- `Current identity`: already true and defensible now
- `Current value`: what the person can realistically help others understand, do, or start
- `Future direction`: what they are growing toward but should not overclaim yet

If the person is early-stage, do not erase their real credentials and track record just to sound humble. Keep real proof visible while preventing over-packaging.

### 3. Define the strategic core

Clarify the following before generating downstream assets:

- Who is the most important audience now
- What concrete problem or tension this audience has
- Why this person cares about that problem
- What perspective or route makes them different
- What proof makes that difference believable
- What should be remembered after first contact

Reduce the brand to a few sharp truths rather than a long identity stack.

### 4. Design the brand structure

Choose and name the structural layers:

- Core brand or main identity
- Signature project, flagship narrative, or proof vehicle
- Sub-brands, side accounts, or adjacent explorations
- Channel roles and ecosystem relationships

When someone has multiple interests or identities, decide whether they are:

- One main brand with branches
- Multiple parallel brands
- One core brand plus supporting expressions

Do not merge everything by default. Only combine lines that strengthen each other without muddying audience understanding.

### 5. Build a usable expression system

Translate the strategy into reusable language:

- One-sentence positioning
- One-sentence value proposition
- Self-introductions at different lengths
- Audience-specific explanations
- Scenario-specific answers
- Red-flag words and overclaim traps

The goal is not “good copy.” The goal is preventing later introductions, bios, websites, and collaboration materials from drifting off-strategy.

### 6. Produce the right deliverable

Match the output to the user's current stage. Common outputs include:

- Brand framework
- Content ecosystem framework
- External expression handbook
- Main/sub-brand relationship brief
- Channel-role map
- Static personal profile or info-card brief

If the user asks for execution before strategy is stable, produce the minimum strategy artifact needed first.

## Working rules

### Prefer truth over polish

If something sounds impressive but is not yet true, rewrite it.

### Preserve stage accuracy

Do not flatten someone into “just exploring” if they already have meaningful credentials, field exposure, or real achievements.

### Avoid generic brand jargon

Words like “expert,” “thought leader,” “empowering others,” or “driving transformation” are weak unless tied to concrete proof.

### Use tension, not fluff

Strong brand strategy often emerges from a live tension, such as:

- Large incumbents versus under-served smaller clients
- Technical possibility versus real-world adoption barriers
- Professional authority versus growing identity
- Efficiency versus warmth

Name the tension clearly when it exists.

### Keep channels role-specific

Do not let every platform do every job. Assign roles deliberately.

## Deliverable standards

Good outputs from this skill should usually include:

- A concise summary of the brand logic
- Clear distinction between current truth and future direction
- Audience definition
- Differentiation based on evidence
- Channel or asset relationships when relevant
- Reusable language, not just abstract strategy

When helpful, provide both:

- a strategic document
- a handoff-friendly brief for downstream execution in another conversation
